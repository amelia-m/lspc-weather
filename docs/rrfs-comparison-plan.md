# Plan: comparing the RRFS on an ongoing basis

> Open-Meteo figures in this document are weather data by [Open-Meteo.com](https://open-meteo.com/), under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/), compared as the document describes. See [open-meteo-terms.md](open-meteo-terms.md).

Draft, 2026-11-24, at the maintainer's word ("plan ongoing comparison. roll into other ongoing data comparison workflows"). Steps 1 and 3 to 5 are built (she said to go ahead with 3 to 5 the same day); 2, 6 and 7 are not.

## Why

NOAA replaced the NAM with the Rapid Refresh Forecast System on 2026-11-03, and Open-Meteo says the HRRR "will be discontinued in the future with the release of RRFSv2" (docs/open-questions.md, "The RRFS"). The dashboard's winds aloft, drift, the Winds aloft card's Surface row and the second cloud figure on Ceiling & sky all come from one Open-Meteo request that names no model and has been served from the HRRR here. When Open-Meteo changes that default, those figures change with no change to this repository. The comparison is there to know, before that day, how large the change is, and to notice the day.

## What it should answer

1. **How far does the winds-aloft table move,** altitude by altitude, and is the difference steady or does it follow the weather or the hour of day?
2. **Which 10 m wind is nearer KPMV's reports,** the default's or the RRFS's? The Surface row is the one row that can be held to a measurement.
3. **How old is the forecast a reader gets?** The first record had the RRFS run served at 04Z as the 18Z run, available nine hours after its start; the HRRR's is hourly (docs/run-split.md).
4. **What does the missing 180 m wind cost?** The RRFS served 10, 80 and 120 m but not 180 m on 2026-11-24, so its 500 ft row rests on fewer samples.
5. **Does its cloud cover differ,** since the card shows that figure as served?
6. **When does the default switch?**

## Steps, in order

Each rolls into a workflow that already runs, on GitHub's runners, never the sandbox. Costs are Open-Meteo calls a day at the weights in docs/open-meteo-terms.md.

1. **Winds aloft, in the comparison (done 2026-11-24, PR #317).** `scripts/schulzeCompare.live.ts` logs the RRFS table minus the default at the same hour, with the RRFS run's metadata (`rrfs` on the record), nine runs a day. The first record from a runner, 2026-11-24 04:06Z (run 45323976716): 22 samples, all 15 rows, the 18Z RRFS run. About 90 calls a day. Answers 1 and 3 once summarised.
2. **The summary and `#parity` (built 2026-11-25, at the maintainer's word, shown as the rows come).** `rrfsSummary` (src/domain/paritySummary.ts) adds the `rrfs` rows up, RRFS minus default: a spread by altitude with the signed mean, the runs with a row over 10° or 3 kt, each model run's age at the comparison, the hours Open-Meteo took to have an RRFS run, the RRFS's sample count and both cloud-cover differences. `#parity` draws it as a panel under the Schulze one (`RrfsPanel`), counts and spreads with the count of runs first, no grade. The published file gains the block at the daily summary's next run. No new requests. First reading, 11 runs from 2026-11-24 04:06Z to 11-25 09:45Z (built from the archives and artifacts with the workflow's script, not published): every run had a row over 10° apart and 9 of 11 one over 3 kt; median 5° to 20° apart by row, 67° at 7,000 ft; speed 1 to 3 kt at the median; the RRFS run had started a median 10.7 h before the comparison (7.6 to 12.1) against 2.1 h (1.7 to 2.8) for the HRRR run the default came from, and Open-Meteo had an RRFS run a median 9 h after its start. Two days of one weather pattern: counts, not a finding about the models.
3. **The 10 m wind, in the surface-wind sampler (built 2026-11-24).** `scripts/surfaceWindCompare.live.ts` already asks once more with `models=ncep_hrrr_conus` and logs whether the default equals it (`sameAsHrrr`). It now makes the same request of the RRFS: its 10 m series as a fourth forecast source beside `nws`, `omHourly` and `om15`, paired against KPMV by `pairsVsMetar`, and the RRFS domain's run metadata beside the HRRR's. The request is three surface variables, weight 1; the archive's recent days hold about 140 runs, so about 140 calls a day. Answers 2 and 3, and feeds the sample section of `#wind-errors` through the weekly refresh as a fifth row, "Open-Meteo RRFS hourly, from 2026-11-24". Records are version 3 (`rrfs`, `om.sameAsRrfs`). First record, from the sandbox at 05:33Z: RRFS 40° 2.7 kt against the default's 46° 2.2 kt and KPMV's 080° 3 kt; same as the HRRR, not the RRFS.
4. **The switch detector (built 2026-11-24).** `defaultModelByDay` counts, per UTC day, the runs whose default differed from the HRRR by name, matched the RRFS by name, or could not get the HRRR by name at all; `defaultModelSwitched` calls a day when any of the three holds for more than half of at least twenty runs. `scripts/defaultModelCheck.ts` runs daily in parity-summary.yml, on `main` only, and opens one issue, labelled `model-switch`, for a change: the notice is given for the change's first seven days (`DEFAULT_MODEL_NOTICE_DAYS`), so a missed run does not lose it, and no issue is opened while one with the label has been opened since the change began (the review of 2026-11-24; the step run against a stub `gh` in six cases, the real `gh issue list --search` not tried). Run over the archive's 47 days to 2026-11-23, no day reads as a switch: the most that differed was 15 of 180, the most HRRR failures 13 of 137. No new requests. Answers 6.
5. **Cloud cover (built 2026-11-24).** The comparison's RRFS answer already carries `cloud_cover` and its three bands, since it is the app's own request; the record now holds those four figures for the hour beside the default's (`rrfs.cloud`), as served. First record: both 100%, low cloud 0% against 5%. No new requests. Answers 5. Read 2026-11-25 over the nine runs that logged both: the RRFS's total cloud cover was 100% in all nine, where the default's ran from 17% to 100% (the same in five, 30 to 83 points under in four); its low cloud was 100% in six, the default's under 26% in all but one. Whether Open-Meteo's RRFS cloud cover saturates or those hours were overcast in that model is not checked; KPMV's sky for those hours would settle it.
6. **The 500 ft row (read 2026-11-25; on `#parity` as a line that moves with the log).** Over the same 11 runs the RRFS served 22 samples every time and the 500 ft row was a median 5° and 1 kt from the default's (at most 23° and 2 kt), against 10° and 1 kt at the surface and 10° and 2 kt at 1,000 ft: it moved less than its neighbours, so the missing 180 m sample shows no cost yet. Eleven runs; the panel's line carries the count. Answers 4, so far.
7. **The archive by lead, on `#wind-errors` (built 2026-11-25).** Both of Open-Meteo's archives serve `models=ncep_rrfs_conus`: a runner fetched its 10 m wind, gust and direction from 2026-04-01 and got hours from 2026-09-26 (Historical Forecast API, 1,333 hours to 11-22) and 09-27 (Previous Runs API's `_previous_day1`, 1,285), run 45603101195, kept as `data/parity/om-wind-rrfs-2026-09-26-to-2026-11-22.jsonl.gz`. `PARTS=omwind` now fetches the default and then the RRFS by name (records tagged `model`; `omwindrrfs` is the RRFS alone), so the weekly refresh keeps both, and its gate holds each half to its own newest hour (`ceilingRefresh.ts fetched omwind`; a made-up week without RRFS records failed it by name, one with both passed). The page's new section (`omRrfsArchiveSection`) sets each RRFS row above the default model's over the same hours, so a pair differs by the model alone. First reading, 2026-09-26 to 11-22 against KPMV (figure: docs/img/om-rrfs-archive-wind-errors-2026-11-22.svg): at the start of run, speed RRFS median +2 kt, mean +1.8 (919 above, 222 below of 1,314 hours) against the default's +1, +0.6 (680, 415); gust +5 kt both (means +5.3 and +5.4, 306 hours); direction median +11°, mean +10.3 against +6°, +4.3 (1,226). A day earlier: speed +2, +1.8 against +1, +1.4 (1,266); direction +13° both (means +14.5 and +13.1). Two months at one station eleven miles from the field; part of every difference is the distance. Answers 2 and adds to 3.
